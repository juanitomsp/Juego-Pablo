# Rules of El Pablo

**El Pablo** (or **Paulo**) is a traditional card game from eastern Andalusia (Spain). It has been played for at least two centuries in Níjar (Almería) and since the 1930s in Mairena del Alcor (Seville), where it arrived with emigrants from Almería.

This document describes **the rules as this game applies them**. Where oral tradition leaves something open, it is marked with «📌 Decision».

The game's own terms (Pablo, Tuerto, Andorra, Melilla, muestra, cuerno, al quiero, arrastre, renuncio…) are kept in Spanish, as they are used at the table.

---

## 1. Players and teams

- **4 or 6 players**, in **two teams**.
- Team members sit **alternately** around the table.
- Play goes **to the right** (counter-clockwise).

## 2. The deck

The Spanish deck is used (1–7, Jack = 10, Knight = 11, King = 12). Suits: coins (oros), cups (copas), swords (espadas) and clubs (bastos).

| Players | Removed | Cards in play |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 | 28 |
| 6 | 8 and 9 | 40 |

## 3. Card values

The **muestra** (turn-up card) is turned face up after dealing. Its suit is **trumps** for that hand.

**Trumps** are: every card of the muestra's suit, plus two cards that are always trumps, whatever the muestra:

- the **Pablo**: the four of clubs.
- the **Tuerto**: the Knight of coins.

Order of trumps, from highest to lowest:

| # | Card | Name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Four of clubs | **Pablo** |
| 2 | Three of trumps | **Andorra** |
| 3 | Knight of coins | **Tuerto** |
| 4 | Two of trumps | **Melilla** |
| 5 | King of trumps | |
| 6 | Knight of trumps (if trumps are not coins) | |
| 7 | Jack of trumps | |
| 8 | Ace of trumps | |
| 9–12 | Seven, six, five and four of trumps (*low viras*) | |

The low 7, 6 and 5 only exist in the 6-player game. The four of trumps only counts if trumps are not clubs (if they are, that four is the Pablo).

Cards that are **not** trumps, from highest to lowest: **King, Knight, Jack, Ace, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2**. Note: outside trumps, the 3 and 2 are the lowest cards.

> Example: with **clubs** as trumps, the Knight of coins is a trump (the Tuerto): it beats the King of coins and every club except the 3 of clubs (Andorra) and the Pablo.

> 📌 **Decision:** the Pablo and the Tuerto always belong to the trump suit. If the muestra itself is the Pablo, trumps are clubs; if it is the Tuerto, coins.

## 4. Goal and scoring

- Each **hand** has 5 tricks: the team that takes **3 tricks** wins the hand.
- A hand without bets is worth **1 point**.
- The first team to reach **8 points** wins a **cuerno** (also called a *partida*). Then both scores go back to 0.
- **2 cuernos** make a **Pablo**.
- **The team that wins a Pablo wins the game**, that is, **two games of 8 points** before the other team.

> 📌 **Decision:** points above 8 are lost. All these numbers (8 points, 2 cuernos, 1 Pablo) can be changed when creating the room, for longer or shorter games.

## 5. Playing a hand

### 5.1 Drawing teams (optional)

If teams have not been decided, cards are dealt face up one by one; whoever gets a **King** stops receiving cards. The first 2 (with 4 players) or 3 (with 6) to get a King form a team, and the very first one is the first **dealer** (*mano*). Online, the «Draw teams by kings» button does this. Otherwise, the first dealer is chosen at random.

### 5.2 The dealer and the deal

- The **dealer** (*mano*) deals. In the next hand, the player to their **right** deals.
- Each player gets **5 cards**, starting to the dealer's right.
- **With 4 players:** the muestra is the card after the last one dealt; it stays face up in the middle with the rest of the deck on top. The cards left in the deck are **asleep** (*dormidas*): they take no part in the hand.
- **With 6 players:** the muestra is the **last card of the deck** and **belongs to the dealer**: it is visible and, in the first trick, the dealer may play it or keep it and play another card. The dealer also **holds back** cards: first to the opponent on their right and to their own partner; when that opponent has played, to the next opponent and the next partner; and when that second opponent has played, to the last opponent (and to themselves).

> 📌 **Decision (online):** the trump suit is always shown on the scoreboard, also with 6 players, although at a real table you must remember it.

### 5.3 Tricks

- The player to the **dealer's right** leads. The last to play is the **postre**; in the first trick, the postre is the dealer.
- The **highest trump** wins the trick. If nobody played a trump, the highest card of the suit led wins.
- The winner of the trick leads the next one.
- **The hand ends as soon as it is decided:** if a team already has 2 tricks and plays a card nobody can beat in the next one, it wins the hand at once, even if others have not played. For example, at 2-2, whoever plays the Pablo wins the hand directly.

> 📌 **Decision:** to decide whether a card «can no longer be beaten», only cards everyone has seen count (cards played and the muestra). Cards in the deck count as possible, so closing early never reveals what anyone holds. So the Andorra only closes the hand if the Pablo has already been played.

**Which card may be played:**

- **Any card, at any time.** You do not have to follow suit or play higher.
- Exception, the **arrastre**: if the first player of the trick leads a **trump** (any card in the trump order, including the Pablo and the Knight of coins), everyone else must play a **trump**, higher or lower.
  - The **Pablo** is the only trump that **may be kept**: if it is your only trump, you may play any other card.
  - The **Tuerto** must be played.
  - If you have no trumps, play any card.
- **Renuncio** (revoke): if you have a trump during an arrastre and do not play it, **your team loses the hand** (the other team gets what the hand is worth at that moment).

> 📌 **Online:** when it is your turn, cards that would win the trick are marked with ✓, and an arrastre is announced. With **Hints** on, cards that would be a revoke are dimmed and ask for confirmation; without hints, the revoke is applied directly. Bots never revoke.

*Avarear de grande* means leading a high trump (Pablo or Tuerto) to force the others to give up trumps; *avarear de chica*, leading a low one.

## 6. Betting

- You bet **on your turn, before playing a card**.
- The **first bet** of a hand can be made, at the earliest, by the **postre in the first trick** (with 6 players, by the **second** player to play). If a team is al quiero, betting is allowed from the start.
- Each bet **raises** the value of the hand by **1 point**: if the first is accepted, the hand goes from 1 to **2**; the next, to **3**; and so on, up to 8 (a whole cuerno).
- After a bet, the other team must answer:
  - **Quiero (accept):** the hand is now worth the bet and play continues.
  - **No quiero (refuse):** the hand ends and the betting team gets **what it was worth until then** (without the refused raise).
- **One bet per turn:** each time it is your turn you may bet 1 (you cannot raise 2 or more at once). If it is your turn again in the same hand, you may bet again; so may any other player, from either team.

> Example: the hand is worth 1. I bet: if they accept, it is worth 2; if they refuse, I win 1. I cannot raise again on this turn. Next time it is my turn I can bet again: if they accept, it is worth 3; if they refuse, I win 2.

- **You cannot abandon a hand:** it is always played to the end. The only things that end a hand early are a refused bet (or a revoke).

> 📌 **Decision:** any player of the team that received the bet may answer (they can discuss it in the chat first); the first answer counts.

## 7. Talking at the table

No signals are used. To decide as a team whether to accept a bet (or just to chat), there is a **💬 Chat** that **all players** read, including the other team. As at a real table, you may talk about the cards you hold, but everyone hears it.

## 8. Special situations

### 8.1 Al quiero

A team is **al quiero** when winning one hand is enough to win the cuerno (with the default numbers, from **7 points**).

- After the deal, the players of that team **show each other their trumps** and decide whether to **play** or **fold**.
- If they fold, the other team gets **1 point**.
- If they play, betting is allowed from the start.
- With 6 players, when a team is al quiero all cards are dealt at once (no holding back).
- **Forced quiero:** if both teams are al quiero, the hand is played without betting; whoever wins it takes the cuerno.

> 📌 **Decision (online):** all players of the al quiero team see their partners' trumps during that hand, and the first to tap «We play» or «We fold» decides.

> 📌 **Decision:** the *carteta* (one-against-one hands) found in some versions is not played: the normal team hand is always played.

## 9. Strategy tips

- A trump wins at most one trick: it is not always wise to keep the big ones until the end.
- When you lead trumps, your partners also give up theirs.
- With 4 players there may be **sleeping** trumps in the deck.
- With 6, in the first trick, the dealer's opponents usually try to play a card higher than the muestra.
- If the other team is al quiero, bet every turn: for them, any hand won is the cuerno.
- When someone complains speculating about the cards the opponents had, the answer is: «**¡Ver dío!**».

## 10. Glossary

- **Andorra:** the three of trumps.
- **Arrastre:** leading a trump; forces the others to play trumps (except the Pablo).
- **Renuncio:** not playing a trump in an arrastre while holding one; the team loses the hand.
- **Cuerno (or partida):** won when reaching 8 points.
- **Dormido:** a trump left in the deck that does not play.
- **Melilla:** the two of trumps.
- **Mano:** the dealer; also, each complete deal of the game.
- **Muestra:** the turn-up card that sets the trump suit.
- **Pablo:** the four of clubs; two cuernos; and the game itself.
- **Postre:** the last player to play in a trick.
- **Triunfo / vira:** a card of the muestra's suit, or the Pablo or the Tuerto.
- **Tuerto:** the Knight of coins.
